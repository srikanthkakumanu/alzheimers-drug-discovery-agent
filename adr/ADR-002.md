# ADR-002 — Evidence provenance and separation of synthetic data

**Status: accepted** 2026-11-09 — accepted by the project owner before implementation · type: `data-architecture`

## Context
Published literature and invented candidate/experiment examples coexist. A citation alone can falsely lend authority to a fictional measurement. Abstract access also differs from full-method appraisal.

## Decision
Represent provenance at record level and in visible text. Keep primary observations, external source summaries, inference, and proposed work distinguishable. Preserve source identifiers, access scope, and negative findings; never promote synthetic records into real experimental evidence.

## Alternatives
| Option | Tradeoff |
| --- | --- |
| Unlabeled combined knowledge base | Convenient retrieval, but makes evidence origin ambiguous. |
| Empty templates only | Avoids invented measurements, but cannot exercise artifact and replication-review examples. |

## Consequences
Reviewers can trace claim origins and reject invalid transfers. Maintaining labels and reciprocal references adds editorial work; automated checks cannot establish that a scientific claim is true.

## Validation
Existing validation checks labels, identifiers, and some references. Human review checks whether the cited source actually supports the claim and whether a result is fictional, measured, or proposed.

## Revisit
Reassess before introducing real laboratory data, private datasets, patient information, or full-text redistribution.
